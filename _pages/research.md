    <!-- Research Overview -->
    <div style="margin-bottom: 40px;">
      <h2 style="color: #333; margin-bottom: 20px;">Research Overview</h2>
      <p style="line-height: 1.6; margin-bottom: 20px;">
        My research interests lie in the intersection of <strong>problem solving</strong>, <strong>strategy selection</strong>, and <strong>individual differences</strong>. I investigate how people choose and adapt their problem-solving strategies, and what cognitive factors influence these choices.
      </p>
      
      <div style="background-color: #f8f9fa; padding: 20px; border-radius: 8px; border-left: 4px solid #007bff; margin-bottom: 20px;">
        <h4 style="color: #333; margin-top: 0; margin-bottom: 15px;">Current Research Focus</h4>
        <ol style="margin: 0; line-height: 1.6;">
          <li><strong>Task Representation & Strategy Selection:</strong> How does task representation affect strategy selection in problem solving?</li>
          <li><strong>Individual Differences:</strong> How does strategy selection associate with individual differences in cognitive abilities?</li>
          <li><strong>Cognitive Mechanisms:</strong> What are the underlying cognitive mechanisms and practical consequences of strategy switching?</li>
        </ol>
      </div>
    </div>

      <div style="border: 1px solid #dee2e6; border-radius: 8px; padding: 20px; margin-bottom: 20px;">
        <h3 style="color: #5bc0de; margin-bottom: 10px; font-size: 1.1em;">
          Task representation and individual differences affect strategy selection and problem-solving performance
        </h3>
        <p style="color: #6c757d; margin-bottom: 10px; font-style: italic;">
          <strong>Xinyu Xie</strong>, Jarrod Moss • Frontiers in Psychology • March 2025
        </p>
        <p style="line-height: 1.5; margin-bottom: 10px;">
          This study investigates how task representation and individual differences affect strategy selection and problem-solving performance. Our findings contribute to understanding the cognitive mechanisms underlying strategic choices in problem-solving contexts.
        </p>
        <div style="font-size: 0.9em;">
          <a href="https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1445200/full" target="_blank" style="color: #007bff; margin-right: 15px;">[Article]</a>
          <span style="background-color: #dc3545; color: white; padding: 2px 8px; border-radius: 12px; font-size: 0.8em;">Front. Psychol.</span>
        </div>
      </div>
      
      <!-- Publication 2 -->
      <div style="border: 1px solid #dee2e6; border-radius: 8px; padding: 20px; margin-bottom: 20px;">
        <h3 style="color: #5bc0de; margin-bottom: 10px; font-size: 1.1em;">
          Comparing Theories that Posit A Role for Task Features in Strategy Selection
        </h3>
        <p style="color: #6c757d; margin-bottom: 10px; font-style: italic;">
          <strong>Xinyu Xie</strong>, Jarrod Moss • CogSci 2024 • Rotterdam, Netherlands
        </p>
        <p style="line-height: 1.5; margin-bottom: 10px;">
          We examined two theories, RCCL and Rational Metareasoning, both incorporating task features into strategy selection models. Using the Building Sticks Task, we found that neither theory aligned closely with empirical data.
        </p>
        <div style="font-size: 0.9em;">
          <a href="/assets/pdf/Cogsci_Poster_2024_final.pdf" target="_blank" style="color: #007bff; margin-right: 15px;">[PDF]</a>
          <span style="background-color: #007bff; color: white; padding: 2px 8px; border-radius: 12px; font-size: 0.8em;">CogSci</span>
        </div>
      </div>
      
      <!-- Publication 3 -->
      <div style="border: 1px solid #dee2e6; border-radius: 8px; padding: 20px; margin-bottom: 20px;">
        <h3 style="color: #5bc0de; margin-bottom: 10px; font-size: 1.1em;">
          Interaction Between Recency Effect and Strategy Preferences in Problem Solving
        </h3>
        <p style="color: #6c757d; margin-bottom: 10px; font-style: italic;">
          <strong>Xinyu Xie</strong>, Jarrod Moss • Psychonomic Society 2023 • San Francisco, CA
        </p>
        <p style="line-height: 1.5; margin-bottom: 10px;">
          Investigated how recency of strategy use affects strategy selection in problem solving. Found that recency effects vary depending on whether the recent strategy matches initial preferences.
        </p>
        <div style="font-size: 0.9em;">
          <a href="/assets/pdf/posterPsychonomics23.pdf" target="_blank" style="color: #007bff; margin-right: 15px;">[PDF]</a>

      <!-- Publication 4 -->
      <div style="border: 1px solid #dee2e6; border-radius: 8px; padding: 20px; margin-bottom: 20px;">
        <h3 style="color: #5bc0de; margin-bottom: 10px; font-size: 1.1em;">
          Working Memory Capacity Predicts More Effective Problem Space Exploration
        </h3>
        <p style="color: #6c757d; margin-bottom: 10px; font-style: italic;">
          <strong>Xinyu Xie</strong>, Jarrod Moss • CogSci 2022 • Toronto, Canada
        </p>
        <p style="line-height: 1.5; margin-bottom: 10px;">
          Examined why some people explore problem spaces more effectively. Found that higher working memory capacity is associated with more unique attempts, faster solution times, and better self-regulation.
        </p>
